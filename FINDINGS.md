# Findings

What the first experiments on this page showed, kept short. The experiments, their instruments,
the nineteen runs and the full report with every figure traced to a file are at tag `0.4`
(`git show 0.4:FINDINGS.md`); tag `0.5` holds the same instruments carried onto the present
protocol and viewer.

The data are small: three repeats at best, one judge, one passage per experiment, and a full cast
of seven readers in only the first two. Read each line as a first measurement, not a result.

## What held up

- **The noise floor.** The same seven readers on the same passage (Mill, *Principles* II.1 §1),
  three times: about half of what one run says in its blind first round is said again in another
  (mean pairwise overlap of readings 0.48, range 0.41 to 0.57), and 55% of all readings appear in
  one run only. A reading found in one run and not another is not evidence of anything until it
  beats these figures.
- **Most recurrence is a model repeating itself.** Of 18 readings found in two or more Mill runs,
  14 recur with the same model in both. The overlap figures measure each model's stability from
  run to run as much as agreement between models.
- **A known text is glossed in stock phrasing.** Under a strict test of sameness, Mill keeps 0.42
  overlap between runs in the blind round and an obscure passage (Samuel Read, 1829) keeps 0.04;
  under the calibrated test the two cannot be told apart. One pair of Read runs.
- **Summaries overreach on what is settled.** Three models each sorted a page into disputed,
  settled and unresolved. Four of the five items a judge found distorted were marked settled:
  the comments agreed on part and left the rest open, and the summary reported a consensus.
- **The page can correct a checkable error.** A planted comment had Blackstone allowing necessity
  to excuse a starving man's theft. Two of the four readers still running caught it in the next
  round, citing *Commentaries* IV.2 correctly. One slip: an existence proof, not a catch rate.

## Thinner

- **The page made rulings more alike.** Five models ruling on six hypothetical cases agreed 0.87
  on the bare passage and 0.93 with the page before them, and the majority flipped on one case.
  More alike is not more correct.
- **A reader joining a finished page glosses the passage and cites the margin.** Twelve of its
  thirteen comments were new glosses listing three or four earlier comments; its one reply
  corrected an attribution that was right.
- **Readers forecast which comments draw replies better than chance** (precision 0.44 and 0.41
  against 0.23 and 0.10), but a third of the hits were comments the forecaster then answered
  itself; without those, 0.30 and 0.27.

## What undercuts them

- After the second experiment the shared providers ran out of credit, and the later ones ran
  with three or four readers.
- Readers did not all write the same way: six called a tool once per comment, in up to sixteen
  steps a turn, and Hermes 4 wrote one reply of tagged blocks.
- Hermes 4 repeated its own earlier comments word for word 54 times in 158 comments, across five
  runs; no other reader did so once. Those repeats are counted in the figures above.

Any of this worth building on needs running again: every reader on one protocol, a full cast,
and more than three repeats.
